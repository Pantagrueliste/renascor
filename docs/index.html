1000 ans de littérature d'oc (Université Ouverte des Humanités): Renaissance excerpts

Occitan · 1567-1617 · html

Short excerpts of Occitan Renaissance authors (Garros, Bellaud de la Bellaudièra, Larade, Ader, Godolin) from the online anthology 1000 ans de littérature d'oc. Occitan Wikisource texts harvested alongside it have their own entry.

1641 Depositions (Trinity College Dublin)

English · 1641-1653 · html

Transcripts, with page images, of the witness statements (depositions and examinations) collected after the Irish rising of October 1641, bound in 32 volumes (TCD MSS 809-840) and kept in Trinity College Library. Every transcript is readable online without login. The project page says the transcribing and online publication ran from 2007 to September 2010 and that the site presents about 8,000 depositions, examinations and associated materials. All the depositions are dated 1641-1653, within 1450-1700. The site's content pages were last changed on 2020-10-16 (WordPress page records), so it is marked archived. Only a TEI-compliant encoding is mentioned on the project pages; no download of the files was found. The counts rest on the site's own listing of 6,932 deposition records, and the texts themselves could not be read: the site answers automated requests with a reCAPTCHA challenge.

ABaC:us — Austrian Baroque Corpus

German · 1680-1693 · tei

Digital corpus of German baroque literary texts (c. 1650-1750), focused on devotional literature around death preparation (Ars moriendi, Totentanz, Memento mori) connected with Abraham a Sancta Clara (1644-1709), e.g. Mercks Wienn (1680), Grosse Todten Bruderschaft (1681). First-edition principle, XML-TEI P5 with linguistic annotation (tokenisation, PoS tagging, lemmatisation) and person/place indices. Project directed by Claudia Resch and Ulrike Czeitschner; online since 2015, new infrastructure launched 2025. Project archive page: https://www.oeaw.ac.at/acdh/research/literary-studies/research/project-archive/abacus.

ABU — Bibliothèque Universelle

French · 1480-1693 · html

Free-access collection of French-language public-domain texts, in the catalogue's early-modern part including works such as the Lettres de la religieuse portugaise, Argens's Lettres juives and Corneille's plays. It is produced and distributed by volunteer members of the Association des Bibliophiles Universels (ABU, a 1901-law association founded in April 1993) and hosted by the CEDRIC multimedia and human-computer interaction team at the Conservatoire national des arts et metiers, Paris. The texts are plain HTML pages for reading online; the catalogue lists 288 texts by 101 authors (January 2002), and the site has had no visible additions since then.

Acta Pacis Westphalicae digital (APW digital)

German, French, Latin · 1643-1649 · tei

Digital edition of the Acta Pacis Westphalicae, the documents of the Westphalian peace congress (instructions, correspondence, minutes, negotiation papers and diaries; the congress ran 1643-1649, with a few earlier or later items). All volumes of the three series are readable online with a document-by-document view, scans of the printed pages, PDF download of the printed volumes and the TEI XML full text of each document (served as TEI fragments, without a teiHeader). No login is needed. The edition text is a digitised critical edition of the archival originals (about 157 archives). The counts cover 3,210 of the 14,545 listed documents (22%), from 19 of the 41 volumes; the server was unstable during the harvest.

Acts and Ordinances of the Interregnum

English · 1642-1659 · html

The full text of the legislation passed into law during the Interregnum (1642-1660), over 900 acts and ordinances, from the edition by C. H. Firth and R. S. Rait (London, 1911, three volumes). British History Online presents the three volumes together, with tables of acts for each year and the editors' introduction. The text was digitised by double rekeying and is free to read on the site. British History Online is a not-for-profit digital library run by the Institute of Historical Research, University of London.

ADL Danish

Danish · 1543-1681 · tei

Texts of the Archive for Danish Literature (ADL), a collaboration between the Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab (DSL) and the Royal Danish Library (KB), published in the public GitHub repository kb-dk/public-adl-text-sources. The corpus comprises 498 volumes (165,512 pages) of Danish literature, all encoded in TEI; the repository contains the roughly two-thirds of pages that have been OCR'd and encoded, one TEI-XML file per volume under texts/. The repository also documents the encoding practices, offers XSLT scripts to extract single works, and accepts contributions by pull request; it was last pushed to in August 2026.

Alciato's Book of Emblems: The Memorial Web Edition in Latin and English

Latin · 1531-1621 · html

Web edition of the 212 emblems of Alciato's Emblematum liber, with the Latin text taken from the 1621 variorum edition and checked against the 1531, 1534 and 1546 editions, an English translation, commentary files and images. The original address (www.mun.ca/alciato) now returns 404, so the URL is the Internet Archive copy of 3 October 2022; the site was last updated in April 2005. The site also carries images of Whitney's Choice of Emblemes (1586) and a 17th-century jest book. The counts cover the Latin text file only, not the English text, the commentaries or those two other works.

ALDICAM-CM — Atlas lingüístico diacrónico e interactivo de la Comunidad de Madrid

Spanish, Latin · 1250-1897 · html

Transcribed archival documents from the 13th to the 19th century from archives of the Comunidad de Madrid (Archivo Regional, Archivo de la Villa, municipal, ecclesiastical and private archives), with paleographic and critical presentation and facsimiles, searchable and mappable without login. Same search engine as CODEA+ 2022; about a third of the regestos coincide with CODEA+ 2022 documents. No bulk download found. About a third of its document summaries coincide with CODEA+ 2022. Twelve documents without a date and three without paleographic text are not in the counts.

Amadis in Translation (Digital Amadis)

Spanish · 1547-1547 · tei

Digital study of the transformation of Montalvo's Amadís de Gaula across editions and translations (Spanish, French, English, from the 1500s to the early 1800s). The corpus files are the 35 TEI XML chapters of the 1547 Sevilla printing of Montalvo's Amadís (facsimile source: Biblioteca Virtual de Andalucía), used to align Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo's text with Robert Southey's 1803 translation and study translation shifts. Project team: Stacey Triplette, Elisa Beshero-Bondar, Helena Bermúdez Sabel; maintained by Beshero-Bondar since 2015. Code and TEI data are on GitHub (AGPL-3.0), with an archived release on Zenodo. Project documentation and a 1533 frontispiece facsimile on the project site.

Archivio Tommaso Campanella (ATC)

Italian, Latin · 1595-1635 · tei

Archivio Tommaso Campanella (ATC), part of the ILIESI 'Archivio dei filosofi del Rinascimento' (with the Bruno and Vanini archives). It gathers the writings of Tommaso Campanella (1568-1639) in text-with-facsimile form, mostly following modern critical editions (for example by Luigi Firpo and Germana Ernst), together with a detailed chronology, a census of early Campanella prints in Italian and foreign libraries, documents, bibliographic notes, studies, images and an ethical-political lexicon. The texts are also available as TEI XML files. Run by ILIESI-CNR (Rome) under the scientific direction of Eugenio Canone; the site is dedicated to Germana Ernst and its pages carry copyright dates 2009-2020.

Athanasius Kircher an Herzog August den Jüngeren: Lateinische Briefe der Jahre 1650-1666 (Editiones Electronicae Guelferbytanae 1)

Latin · 1650-1666 · html

Transcription of the nineteen Latin letters Athanasius Kircher sent to Duke August the Younger of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel (Herzog August Bibliothek, BA II 351-365 and others), with line numbers, a transcription with apparatus, a German translation, commentary and source images. Published in 2003; the revision log shows the last changes in December 2012 and 2018 (link corrections), so the status is archived. HAB terms allow private scholarly use and bar commercial reuse. The counts cover the 21 letters found on the transcription page.

Balassi Bálint összes verse: hálózati kritikai kiadás (network critical edition of the complete poems)

Hungarian · 1570s-1594 · html

Critical edition of Balassi's poems, first published online in 1998 (version 1.0), revised as 1.1 (1999) and 2.0 (2002), with facsimiles, apparatus, notes and a repertory. The site is a set of HTML frames. Newest file seen was modified in 2006. The poems carry no individual dates, so their place within 1450-1700 rests on the author's life span (he died in 1594).

Balkan Slavic Digital Editions (Pop Punčov Sbornik, Life of St. Petka, Tale of Alexander, Excerpts)

Church Slavonic, Bulgarian · 10th-19th century (sources of 1450-1700 among them) · json

Corpus of Balkan Slavic texts from the 10th to the 19th century collected for the study of linguistic change: Pop Punčo's Sbornik (1796), the Life of St. Petka in many copies, the Tale of Alexander the Elder, and an Excerpts sub-corpus. Texts come with English translations, morphological and syntactic annotation, search and facsimiles. Sources within 1450-1700 include Vuković's Zbornik za putnike (printed 1536), the Joseph-Volokolamsk manuscript of 1537, the Tikhonravov damaskin and other 17th-century damaskini, Šimun Kozičić (1530s), and Letters of Wallachian princes. Texts are read in the web interface. The search engine loads its corpus from public token-level JSON files with diplomatic form, lemma, MULTEXT-East tags and Universal Dependencies relations (lt_data.json for the Excerpts texts, petka_data.json for the Life of St. Petka, sva_data.json for the Tale of Alexander, data_1.json and data_2.json for Pop Punčo's Sbornik of 1796). These files are not advertised as downloads. Search results can be exported as CSV or CoNLL-U. The 23 Damaskini texts are also deposited at CLARIN.SI (11356/1441). Status active: the home page was modified in July 2026 and the Excerpts page in February 2026. The counts cover the 13 texts dated within 1450-1700, leaving out the Sbornik (1796), and several dates are approximate (century midpoints or decades).

English · 1550-1608 · xml

Complete correspondence of Bess of Hardwick (c. 1521/7-1608): 234 letters to and from her, each with diplomatic and normalised transcripts, colour images of 185 of them, and commentary. BetaMasaheft Geez

Ge'ez · 1555-1555 · tei

TEI-XML records of Ethiopian and Eritrean literary works (Ge'ez and other languages of the Christian written tradition) kept in the BetaMasaheft/Works GitHub repository, the data layer of the Beta masahaft project 'Manuscripts of Ethiopia and Eritrea'. The records are served through the project's eXist-db application at betamasaheft.eu, which links works to manuscripts, persons and places and is built on a Clavis Aethiopica numbering of works. The project is a long-term programme of the Academy of Sciences and Humanities in Hamburg (2016-2040), directed by Alessandro Bausi and hosted by the Hiob Ludolf Centre for Ethiopian and Eritrean Studies, University of Hamburg; it follows an ERC project led by Denis Nosnitsin (2009-2015). The repository is under active development (last push October 2026).

Biblioteca Italiana

Italian, Latin · 1460-1650 · tei

Digital library of texts of Italian literary and cultural tradition from the Middle Ages to the twentieth century, promoted since 1996 by the Centro Interuniversitario Biblioteca Italiana Telematica (CIBIT) and run from the Department of Italianistica e Spettacolo at Sapienza University of Rome; the BIBIT section was started under Amedeo Quondam in 2003. BIBIT holds more than 1,600 works in full-text editions based on scholarly reference editions, encoded in XML/TEI (the TEI header records each text's history), which can be read, searched with concordances and downloaded; the site also holds scanned incunabula and the Scrittori d'Italia series as images. The portal's content pages were last edited in 2019.

Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes (BVMC)

Spanish · 1492-1700 · html

Spanish texts harvested from the Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, the digital library of the Universidad de Alicante: comedias and autos by Lope de Vega, Calderón, Tirso de Molina, Moreto, Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz and others, with prose such as Cárcel de amor, La lozana andaluza and Don Quijote. Open access, but individual texts are copyrighted by the BVMC (no blanket open licence).

Bibliotheca Augustana

Latin · 1480-1650 · html

Bibliotheca Augustana, a collection of electronic texts in Latin and in Greek, German, English, French, Italian, Spanish, Polish, Russian, Portuguese, Czech and Yiddish, with a chronological and an alphabetical index for each language section. Bibliothèques Virtuelles Humanistes — Épistémon

French · 1521-1616 · tei

Epistemon is the text database of the Bibliothèques Virtuelles Humanistes (BVH) programme at the Centre d'Études Supérieures de la Renaissance, Université de Tours (head: Rémi Jimenes). It offers Renaissance texts (literature and author corpora, literary manuscripts and 16th-century archival documents) transcribed in a 'patrimonial' manner that respects layout, spelling and punctuation, encoded in XML-TEI following the project's TEI-Renaissance guidelines. The first texts went online in 1998 at the University of Poitiers and moved to Tours in 2001; TEI encoding has been used since 2007. Texts are read and searched through XTF, PhiloLogic and TXM, with facsimiles shown alongside where available. Fidelity levels of older transcriptions are heterogeneous, and the base is still being extended and revised. It includes the Montaigne author corpus of the ANR MONLOE project (montaigne.univ-tours.fr): the genetic edition of the Exemplaire de Bordeaux of the 1588 Essais (also deposited on HAL, halshs-01239978), the 1580 edition of the Essais (books I and II), Montaigne's 1581 translation of Sebond's Théologie naturelle, La Boétie's Servitude volontaire (Bordeaux Ms 2199), letters and family archives; MONLOE's editions of Montaigne's marginalia and inscriptions are PDFs on the BVH server and are not counted. The counts combine two harvests that share no text: 70 texts from the BVH site and 46 printed texts from a legacy TEI copy in Lou Burnard's tei-fr repository on GitHub (last updated 2015).

Bullinger Digital

French, German, Italian, Latin · 1523-1575 · tei

Correspondence of the Zurich reformer Heinrich Bullinger (1504-1575), about 12,000 letters to and from him dated 1523-1575, held mostly in the Staatsarchiv des Kantons Zuerich and the Zentralbibliothek Zuerich. Metadata, summaries and footnotes come from the printed HBBW edition; the letter texts come from the HBBW, other printed editions, the HBBW team's preparatory work and automatic handwriting recognition, and are published with facsimiles, normalised search forms, machine translation into English and linked named entities. The project ran 2020-2025 at the Institute of Computational Linguistics, University of Zurich (leaders Patricia Scheurer, Phillip B. Stroebel, Martin Volk; funded by the UZH Foundation); at its end in May 2025 the TEI-XML corpus was handed to the Staatsarchiv Zuerich, which now maintains the online search system.

CAMENA

Latin · 1500-1699 · tei

Texts are taken from a republication of the project's XML files on GitHub (Neven Jovanović, University of Zagreb, snapshot 2016), licensed CC-BY-SA; the CAMENA site at the Universität Mannheim serves the same material work by work. The project has had no news since 2017.

The Casebooks Project

English · 1596-1640 · tei

Digital edition of the medical casebooks of the astrologers Simon Forman and Richard Napier (1596-1634), whose consultation records are held by the Bodleian Library, Oxford. Each case is transcribed in XML and published with page images, name, place and treatment directories, critical introductions and selected cases in full. It was produced by the Casebooks Project, a team at the University of Cambridge led by Professor Lauren Kassell, with Wellcome Trust funding from 2008 (pilot, programme and strategic awards, 2008-2019); since 2019 the project is in an archiving and preservation phase. The raw XML transcriptions and authority lists are published in the CasebooksProject/casebooks-data GitHub repository, and the images are at the Cambridge Digital Library. The site's pages returned errors when checked on 2026-10-13, so the details come from archived copies.

CELT Corpus of Electronic Texts

English, Irish, Latin · 1454-1698 · tei

Online textbase of Irish history, literature and politics from University College Cork, comprising texts in Irish, Latin, Anglo-Norman French and English, with English translations where possible; a large part dates from before 1700. Texts are taken from the best printed editions, scanned, proofread and encoded in TEI-conformant SGML or XML, with HTML versions and introductions and bibliographies, and can be read, searched and downloaded. CELT was founded in 1992 by Professor Donnchadh O Corrain, who directed it until his retirement; it was funded by the American Ireland Funds, the Irish Higher Education Authority and the Irish Research Council for the Humanities and Social Sciences, among others. The site is still online but its copyright line reads 1997-2010 and the FAQ counts 1638 texts as of 24 February 2021.

CHARTA — Corpus hispánico y americano en la red: textos antiguos

Spanish, Latin · 944-1871 · tei

VAKK Estonian

Estonian · 1490-1700 · html

Corpus of Old Written Estonian (VAKK) of the University of Tartu, with texts from the 15th to the 19th century. It holds all known surviving Estonian texts of the 15th and 16th centuries (manuscripts and prints, name lists excepted), most surviving 17th-century prints and known manuscript sermons, and a selection of 18th- and 19th-century prints; it also hosts 19th-century parish court protocols. Older texts are lemmatised and morphologically tagged, partly by hand and partly with Külli Prillop's Vakker software, and the corpus is searched and filtered online, with CSV or Excel export of results. The work began in 1995 in the Old Written Language research group of the Chair of Estonian (led by Valve-Liivi Kingisepp) and is funded by Estonian national programmes; the corpus has a DOI and is listed in META-SHARE.

About Renascor

Renascor is an open catalogue of digital editions of written documents from about 1450 to 1700: manuscripts, printed works, correspondence and inscriptions. Each record states who made the resource, which languages and years its texts cover, how large it is, how it is encoded and where to find it.

Scope

“Renaissance” is used here as a historical and cultural framework centred on Europe, particularly Western Europe, between 1450 and 1700. The catalogue also includes texts arising from Europe’s contacts with the rest of the world (trade, diplomacy, travel, migration, missions and colonial encounters), written on either side of these exchanges, in any language, and considered within their own traditions.

The catalogue points to resources and hosts no texts. It lists only resources that give a transcribed text, epigraphic editions included. Page images or photographs without transcription, uncorrected OCR and bibliographies are left out.

Why transcribed text?

A facsimile shows a page; a transcription makes its text usable. It can be searched, counted, compared and mined at scale, and it lets students and researchers experiment with the texts themselves: analyse them, annotate them, play with them. It also makes the documents accessible. A screen reader or a braille display can read a transcription but not a page image, and readers can enlarge or reflow it, or read it without first mastering old hands and typefaces.

Why no paywalled resources?

A digital text is most useful when it can be shared, checked, analysed and mined. Paywalls restrict that use and put scholars without institutional means at a disadvantage.

How this catalogue was made

From hand to agentic AI

The catalogue was first compiled by hand, then set aside before the size of the task. It became feasible with agentic AI coding assistants, which over the past year have made it possible to search for resources, check them and refine the records at scale.

Open to everyone

The catalogue lives in a public GitHub repository. Anyone can improve it by opening an issue to propose a resource or a correction. Anyone who disagrees with its criteria can fork it and build a catalogue that follows their own principles.

Automated upkeep

GitHub’s continuous integration and deployment also keep the catalogue maintained: every change is checked against the schema and tested, the site is rebuilt and published automatically, and a weekly search proposes new resources. Upkeep is where earlier catalogues failed. The Renaissance Knowledge Network has been frozen since 2017, and Folgerpedia’s list of digital editions of English Renaissance drama was last edited in 2016. Renascor is built instead as a closed loop: AI agents and contributors propose changes, automated checks test them, a person approves them, the site is republished, and the published catalogue is the starting point for the next round of searches and checks. Because the loop runs on its own, the catalogue can last and keep improving itself.

No grant, no cost

That is the idea behind this project: it had to cost next to nothing to run and need no grant, because cheapness and sustainability go together. The site is hosted free on GitHub Pages, the checks run on GitHub’s free automation, and each weekly search costs a few cents.

Human review

Automation never acts alone. Every change arrives as a pull request, and a person reviews and approves it before it is published.

How records are made

Inclusion rule

Collections that provide freely accessible Renaissance texts are eligible even when their contents overlap. Each collection’s size is counted before content deduplication; deduplication applies separately to the Renascor Corpus. Subscription collections and directories without texts are excluded; exclusions are recorded with their reasons.

Contributions

Anyone can propose a resource or a correction through a form on GitHub, or by pull request. Each record is one JSON file in the public repository, checked against a published schema.

Review

The maintainer reviews every record before publication: that the resource exists, that it provides transcribed text from the period, and that it is described accurately.

Automated search

A weekly search uses Mistral Large 4, chosen for its multilingual capabilities and planned open-weight release, to propose candidate resources as pull requests. Every addition is reviewed by a human.

Further AI agents will be added to maintain the catalogue: checking that links still work, updating each resource’s status, licence and figures when its host changes, and spotting resources that have moved or closed. Like the search, they will only propose changes as pull requests, for a person to review.

Document types

Each resource is classed by the kind of document its texts are: manuscripts (including archival documents and letters), printed works or inscriptions, or several of these when its texts mix them. The Type filter uses this classification.

Countries

Countries are the present-day countries where a resource’s documents were written or printed (ISO 3166 codes; England, Scotland and Wales are kept apart), as defined in schema/countries.json. They appear in each record and can be searched.

Origins and figures

The Renascor Corpus Project

This catalogue grew out of the Renascor Corpus Project, which is assembling as complete a corpus of Renaissance texts as possible for AI training; the corpus has not yet been released. It shows which Renaissance texts can be read online as digital text today.

Word counts

A collection’s word count covers its texts dated 1450–1700, including those also found in other collections. The Renascor Corpus total counts each text once (November 2026). Each record explains how its figures were obtained.

Periods and dates

Periods give the first and last year of the texts: a single year when they coincide, “c.” for an approximate date, and a century counted as its hundred years (16th century = 1500–1599). The period filter keeps any resource whose period overlaps the chosen years; periods given only in words are not matched. The period graph is approximate and includes dates estimated from author lifespans. For 65 resources, the record also gives the host’s last update, such as a GitHub push or a new Zenodo version.
